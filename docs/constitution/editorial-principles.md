# Editorial principles

Status: Draft, awaiting owner ratification

## Entries

1. An entry does not need to be insightful, representative, technically correct or important in the editors' view to have archival value.
2. The contributor's own words are the entry. Structured fields support browsing and research but never replace the free text.
3. Contributing should feel like signing a guestbook, not completing a questionnaire. Prompts are optional aids.
4. Entries are presented as written. The project does not correct, summarize, score or rank them.
5. The archive does not silently classify entries, including by guessing whether AI produced them. Metadata comes from contributors or is labelled with its origin.

## Presentation

6. There is no recommendation algorithm, personalized feed, engagement ranking or "people like you also liked". Visitors choose what to look for: chronological browsing, search and filters.
7. Filters must not combine into granular slices that make anonymous contributors identifiable. A filter appears only when it is populated enough to be useful and safe.
8. Each published entry has a permanent address and a citation that respects the contributor's chosen public identity.
9. No comments, replies, likes, followers, messaging, reputation or public argument threads. An entry may respond to another intellectually; the platform does not build a conversation graph around it.

## Context

10. Contextual material exists to help a future reader understand the testimonies and their world. Its test: does a future reader need this to understand what the contributors were describing?
11. Context is selective, not encyclopedic. It may cover social dimensions that other archives cover poorly, such as labour, education, culture, companionship, hype, backlash and ordinary uses.
12. Context pages are dated and revised openly. They describe the period; they do not argue with the entries.

## Public language

13. The interface speaks plainly: "entry", "sign the guestbook", "read the guestbook", "time capsule". Academic terms such as "testimony", "corpus" or "Zeitzeugnis" belong in methodology pages and internal documentation.
