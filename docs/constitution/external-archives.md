# Relationship to external archives

Status: Draft, awaiting owner ratification

1. **Do not duplicate specialist work.** Where another project preserves something well, such as models, benchmarks, papers, source code, technical artifacts, incidents or institutional reporting, the Guestbook explains why it matters and points to it rather than building a competing collection.
2. **Do not delegate the social record.** The Guestbook remains willing to document social dimensions that are poorly preserved elsewhere when they help readers understand the entries: cultural discourse, labour conflict, education, creative controversies, enterprise adoption, public expectations, companionship, attitudes towards automation, changing language, media narratives, policy disputes, hype and backlash, scams and generated content, ordinary uses and regional differences.
3. **References are records, not bare links.** Each important external resource gets a reference shelf record with enough information to identify it if the link dies: name, role, homepage, resource URL, description, date verified, licence, local snapshot if any, and notes.
4. **Preserve when lawful, useful and proportionate.** Important static resources or datasets may be snapshotted or included in editions when their licence allows. When redistribution is not appropriate, the metadata is still kept.
5. **Reassess periodically.** What other projects preserve well changes over time. The reference shelf is reviewed regularly for disappeared or materially changed resources and for new gaps.
6. **Partnership over competition.** Libraries, archives, universities and other preservation projects are potential custodial partners, mirror operators or depositaries for editions, not rivals.
