# Mission

Status: Ratified (2026-11-20)

The Artificial Intelligence Guestbook is a human time capsule of the AI era.

It preserves first-person accounts of what it is like to live alongside artificial intelligence, written by the people living through it, while the future is still unknown.

## What it preserves

- What people use AI for and what they refuse to use it for.
- What has changed in their work, education, creativity, relationships, habits, expectations and culture.
- What they find useful, exciting, frightening, irritating, boring, absurd, overhyped or underestimated.
- What they expect, predict and fear, recorded before hindsight corrects it.
- Ordinary details that history might otherwise forget.
- Disagreement between people in different places in the same period.

Its subject is people, not technology. Enthusiasm, hostility, indifference, uncertainty and contradiction are preserved side by side without being reconciled into a single account.

## Who may contribute

Anybody. Not only experts, users, particular professions, countries or political positions. "I barely use AI and don't care very much" is as legitimate a testimony as an expert's long analysis. Requirements arising from law, abuse prevention, security or parental consent are operational constraints, not exceptions to this principle.

## What it is not

Not a social network, an encyclopedia of AI, a technology news site, a model database or an engagement platform. Where specialist projects preserve technical history well, the Guestbook points to them.

## The obligation it creates

Accepting testimonies means accepting custody of them: moderating, honouring privacy rights, preserving data, producing editions and keeping the archive intelligible. See [Continuity](continuity.md).

## The test for new features

Does it make it easier to preserve, understand, discover or responsibly maintain human testimony about living through the AI era? Features are also judged by the permanent maintenance burden they create, because the project's scarce resource is custodial attention.
