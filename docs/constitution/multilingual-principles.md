# Multilingual principles

Status: Ratified (2026-11-20)

1. **First-class from the start.** The interface supports at least French and English for every contributor-facing flow: project explanation, contribution form, consent, moderation status, privacy controls, browsing, withdrawal requests, emails and notifications.
2. **Any language may be used for testimonies.** Contributors write in the language they choose. The interface language and the testimony language are independent.
3. **The original is canonical.** The original-language version is the testimony. It is always shown and always available.
4. **Translations never replace the original silently.** A translation is shown as a translation, linked to its original.
5. **Translation provenance is explicit.** Every translation records who or what produced it (a person, a machine translation system with its name and version, or the contributor), when, and whether a person reviewed it. Machine translations are labelled as such.
6. **Fluency is not authority.** Translation can materially alter testimony, so no translation, human or machine, is treated as authoritative.
7. **Editorial attention starts with French and English**, because the maintainer can evaluate those. Crowdsourced or contributor-supplied translations may come later.
8. **Language metadata is accurate.** Pages and testimony text carry correct language tags (BCP 47) for accessibility, search engines and future readers.
9. **Legal and consent texts are equally binding in both languages.** They are written and reviewed in both, not machine-translated without review.
