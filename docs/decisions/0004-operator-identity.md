# 0004. The operator is publicly named

Status: Accepted (2026-11-15)

Issue: #19

## Context

French law requires the publisher of an online service to be identifiable, while allowing non-professional individuals to stay anonymous to the public by giving their identity to their host. The exact provisions are being verified in [the legal research](../research/legal-landscape.md). An archive asks contributors and future readers to trust its custody and provenance.

## Decision

The publisher and custodian is publicly named as Raphaël Bardini in the mentions légales and on the About page.

## Consequences

- Provenance and accountability are clear to contributors, readers and archival partners.
- The operator's name is attached to every moderation outcome. Moderation principles must be public and applied consistently ([constitution](../constitution/moderation-principles.md)).
- Personal contact details published for legal purposes should be limited to what the law requires; a project contact address should be used rather than a personal one.
- Open (found by the legal research, 2026-11-15): LCEN art. 1-1 I 1 requires a natural-person publisher to publish name, domicile and telephone number, unless the non-professional route of art. 1-1 II is used, under which only the host's details are published. Whether a publicly named operator can rely on art. 1-1 II for the mentions légales, or whether a domiciliation address satisfies "domicile", is unresolved. To be settled in #19 before launch.
