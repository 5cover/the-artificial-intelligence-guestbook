# Publishing rules

Status: Draft (2026-11-15; rules and review window accepted by the owner 2026-11-20). Public wording to be finalized in English and French during implementation, and reviewed before launch (#19); it does not block the first prototype.

Basis: [moderation principles](../constitution/moderation-principles.md); [editorial principles](../constitution/editorial-principles.md); [authorship disclosure](../constitution/authorship-disclosure.md); [illegal-content protocol](../operations/illegal-content.md); [moderation-failures research](../research/moderation-failures.md); DSA arts. 14, 16 and 17 as summarized in the [legal research](../research/legal-landscape.md), section 5.

These rules are the only grounds for not publishing a testimony, for returning it to its author for changes, or for removing it later. Each rule has a stable code, which moderation records and statements of reasons cite (data model, section 2.10). The rules are short on purpose: the Guestbook publishes disagreement, error, enthusiasm and hostility alike, and it never rejects a testimony for its opinions.

The draft below is the public text, written to be read by contributors.

## What the Guestbook publishes

Any first-person testimony about living with AI, from anyone, in any language, of any length within the limit, and with any opinion, including mistaken, unpopular or critical ones. That includes criticism of the Guestbook itself. You may use AI to write your testimony, as long as you say how in the authorship question.

Names are not verified. A display name is what the contributor chose to give, and the Guestbook says so wherever it shows one.

## What we do not publish

| Code | Rule | Usual action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `illegal` | Content that is illegal under French or EU law, for example child sexual abuse material, incitement to hatred or violence, or apology of terrorism | Not published, and reported to the authorities where the law requires it |
| `defamation` | Presenting as fact an allegation that damages the honour or reputation of an identifiable person (loi du 29 juillet 1881, art. 29) | We ask you to change the passage, for example to describe your own experience rather than accuse |
| `threat` | Threats against a person or group | Not published; reported if there is a danger to life or safety |
| `harassment` | Content whose purpose is to harass, demean or intimidate an identifiable person | Not published |
| `third-party-privacy` | Private or sensitive information about another identifiable person: their health, address, contact details, workplace, or anything that could locate or expose them | We ask you to change the passage |
| `secrets` | Passwords, keys, access tokens, personal identification numbers or similar secrets, yours or anyone else's | We ask you to remove the secret, and hide the testimony until you do |
| `spam` | Automated, repeated or bulk submissions, or the same text posted again | Not published; repeated cases restrict the account |
| `advertising` | Submissions whose main purpose is to promote a product, service, site or person, or to place links for search engines | Not published, or we ask you to remove the links |
| `not-testimony` | Content that is not a testimony at all, for example random text, a test, or a document pasted without any account of the author's own experience | We ask you to change it, or it is not published |
| `impersonation` | Publishing under the real name of someone else without their agreement | We ask you to use a pseudonym or to publish anonymously |
| `false-disclosure` | Deliberately false answers to the AI-authorship question, as part of automated or bulk submission | Treated as `spam` |

We do not reject a testimony because we disagree with it, find it badly written, think its prediction is foolish, or believe it is wrong about AI.

## How publication works

1. Every testimony is read before it is first published. Nothing is published automatically. Once published, you can edit your testimony at any time; edits appear at once and are read afterwards.
2. Possible outcomes:
   - it is published;
   - we ask you to change something, for example to remove a colleague's name or a phone number. This is common and usually accidental; it is not a sanction. Your text stays available to you, and we read it again as soon as you edit it;
   - it is not published.
3. Except in the cases listed below, you receive the outcome by email. If we ask for a change or do not publish, the email gives the rule code, the reason, what to change, and how to ask for a review.
4. We prefer the smallest change: we would rather ask you to change one name than refuse a whole testimony. We never change your words ourselves.
5. Testimonies from contributors under 18 are read with particular care for personal details.

The exception: when the law or an authority requires discretion, for example during a criminal report, the message can be delayed or kept general.

## Asking for a review

You can ask for a review of any decision within 30 days, by replying to the decision email. We look at the testimony again, with your explanation, and answer with our reasons. A review is never done on the same day as the original decision. Until the Guestbook has a second moderator, the custodian handles reviews; a second reviewer is a decision deferred until after launch (#41; the moderation-failures research recommends it).

## Reporting a published testimony

Anyone can report a published testimony they believe is illegal, or that exposes them or someone else. Use the report link on the testimony page, or write to the project address. A report asks us to look; it does not hide the testimony by itself, and the number of reports does not matter. Tell us:

- which testimony (its address or code);
- why you believe it is illegal or harmful;
- your name and email, so we can answer (not required for reports of child sexual abuse material);
- that you make the report in good faith.

We acknowledge every report that includes contact details, decide promptly, and tell you the outcome (DSA art. 16). How reports are handled, including deadlines and limits, is described in [reports](../specs/reports.md). Knowingly false reports are an offence under French law (LCEN art. 6 VII, to confirm).

## Repeated violations

Accounts that repeatedly break these rules, or submit spam, can be restricted: they can no longer submit. We tell the account holder, give the reason, and explain how to ask for a review. Withdrawal of existing testimonies always remains possible.

## Changes to these rules

These rules change rarely, and only to make them clearer or to follow the law. Every version is dated and kept public. A testimony is judged under the rules in force when it was submitted.
